## Context: Why Payment Processing is Critical for Associations

After working with multiple associations during our field research, we've noticed that payment processing is one of the biggest pain points.
Many associations are still using manual bank transfers, Excel spreadsheets to track who paid, and spending hours chasing members for renewals.
This is exactly the kind of administrative chaos AssoCORE aims to eliminate.

When building a membership platform, you need more than just "accepting payments." You need:

* **Recurring Billing:** Automatic monthly or yearly renewals without manual intervention
* **Self-Service Portals:** Members should be able to update their payment methods, view invoices, and manage their subscriptions without bothering the admin
* **Failed Payment Handling:** A robust system to retry failed payments and notify members before automatically canceling their membership
* **Tax Receipt Generation:** For French associations, this means automatic "Cerfa" forms for tax deductions
* **Non-Profit Pricing:** Every euro saved on transaction fees is money that goes directly to the association's mission
* **Dunning Management:** Automated email sequences when payments fail, with grace periods before access is revoked
* **Compliance:** GDPR-compliant payment data handling, PCI-DSS compliance for storing card tokens

## Deep Dive: Provider Analysis

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Stripe (Our Choice)">
    ### Stripe Billing & Subscriptions

    After prototyping with both providers, **Stripe is our clear winner** for AssoCORE. Here's why:

    #### The API Experience

    Stripe's API is genuinely a joy to work with. Everything is well-documented, the SDK is actively maintained, and their webhook system is bulletproof. We've integrated it into our FastAPI backend in less than a day.

    ```python
    # Creating a subscription is this simple
    subscription = stripe.Subscription.create(
        customer=customer_id,
        items=[{"price": "price_1234"}],
        payment_behavior="default_incomplete",
        payment_settings={"save_default_payment_method": "on_subscription"},
        expand=["latest_invoice.payment_intent"],
    )
    ```

    #### Customer Portal (Game Changer)

    This is the feature that sold us. Stripe provides a **fully-hosted, production-ready portal** where members can:
    - Update their credit card without you storing any sensitive data
    - Download all their invoices
    - Cancel or reactivate their subscription
    - View their payment history

    You literally just redirect to `https://billing.stripe.com/p/session/[SESSION_ID]` and Stripe handles everything. This saved us from building roughly 15 different UI screens.

    <Aside type="tip">
    You can customize the portal's colors and branding to match your association's identity. Configure this in the Stripe Dashboard under Settings → Customer Portal.
    </Aside>

    #### Non-Profit Pricing

    For registered non-profits (loi 1901 associations in France qualify), Stripe offers:
    - **2.2% + €0.25** per transaction (vs. standard 2.9% + €0.30)
    - That's a **24% reduction in fees**
    - For an association processing €10,000/year, this saves roughly €700

    You need to apply through their non-profit program with your association's registration documents.

    #### Webhook Reliability

    Stripe webhooks are enterprise-grade. They:
    - Retry failed deliveries automatically (exponential backoff)
    - Sign every webhook with a secret (preventing spoofing attacks)
    - Provide a dashboard showing every webhook attempt and response

    We use these critical events:
    - `customer.subscription.created` - Grant access to the platform
    - `customer.subscription.deleted` - Revoke access immediately
    - `invoice.payment_failed` - Trigger dunning email sequence
    - `invoice.paid` - Send confirmation and generate receipt

    #### Smart Retries & Dunning

    Stripe's **Smart Retries** use machine learning to automatically retry failed payments at optimal times. According to their data, this recovers about 15-20% of failed payments that would otherwise churn.

    You can configure:
    - How many retry attempts (we use 4 retries over 2 weeks)
    - Email notifications to customers
    - Grace period before cancellation

    #### The Downsides

    **Tax Receipts:** Stripe doesn't generate French "Cerfa" forms automatically. We need to build this ourselves. We're planning to use `pdfkit` or `reportlab` to generate these when the `invoice.paid` webhook fires.

    **Complexity:** While powerful, Stripe has a learning curve. There are dozens of objects (Customer, Subscription, Invoice, Payment Intent, Setup Intent) and understanding the lifecycle takes time.

    **International Cards:** Stripe sometimes blocks cards from certain countries as fraud prevention. This can be frustrating for international members.

  </TabItem>

  <TabItem label="Lemon Squeezy">
    ### Lemon Squeezy: The Merchant of Record Approach

    Lemon Squeezy is fascinating because they fundamentally change the model - they become the **Merchant of Record**, meaning legally *they* are selling the membership, not your association.

    #### How This Works

    When a member pays:
    1. Lemon Squeezy charges their card
    2. Lemon Squeezy remits tax to all relevant jurisdictions
    3. They send you a payout (minus their fee and taxes)
    4. They issue the legally-compliant invoice

    #### The VAT Problem They Solve

    Here's the nightmare scenario Lemon Squeezy prevents:

    If your French association has members across the EU, technically you need to:
    - Register for VAT in every EU country where you have customers
    - File VAT returns in each country quarterly
    - Track changing VAT rates (Ireland is 23%, Germany is 19%, etc.)
    - Handle "reverse charge" for B2B transactions

    Lemon Squeezy handles **all of this** for you. They're registered in every jurisdiction.

    #### The Developer Experience

    Their API is simpler than Stripe's (fewer objects), but also less flexible:

    ```javascript
    // Creating a subscription
    const checkout = await lemonSqueezy.createCheckout({
      storeId: process.env.LEMON_SQUEEZY_STORE_ID,
      variantId: "123456", // Your membership tier
      customPrice: 2999, // €29.99
      checkoutData: {
        email: user.email,
        custom: { userId: user.id }
      }
    });
    ```

    Webhooks are straightforward but less reliable than Stripe's in our testing. We've had occasional delays (5-10 minutes) before webhook delivery.

    #### Beautiful Checkout Flow

    We'll admit: Lemon Squeezy's checkout UI is more polished than Stripe's. It's modern, mobile-optimized, and feels very "2026." If you care about conversion rates on the checkout page, this matters.

    #### The Real Cost

    - **5% + €0.50** per transaction
    - **$50 minimum payout threshold** (painful for small associations just starting)
    - For a €30 membership: You lose €2 to fees (vs. €0.91 with Stripe non-profit pricing)

    For a small association with 100 members paying €30/year:
    - **Lemon Squeezy:** €200 in fees
    - **Stripe (non-profit):** €91 in fees
    - **Difference:** €109/year you're giving away

    #### Our Take

    Lemon Squeezy makes sense if:
    - You're targeting international members across 20+ countries
    - You don't have a legal entity capable of handling tax compliance
    - You value simplicity over cost optimization
    - You're a solo developer building an MVP quickly

    For AssoCORE, where we're primarily serving French associations, the fee premium isn't justified.

## Our Implementation Strategy for AssoCORE

After all this research, here's exactly how we're building the payment integration for AssoCORE:

<Steps>
1.  **Stripe as Primary Payment Provider**

    The architecture is designed with a clean abstraction layer that could support multiple payment providers in the future:

    ```python
    # app/services/payment_service.py
    class PaymentProvider(ABC):
        @abstractmethod
        async def create_subscription(self, user_id: str, plan: str) -> Subscription:
            pass

        @abstractmethod
        async def cancel_subscription(self, subscription_id: str) -> bool:
            pass

    class StripeProvider(PaymentProvider):
        async def create_subscription(self, user_id: str, plan: str):
            # Stripe implementation
            pass
    ```

    This flexible architecture allows us to add alternative providers later without major refactoring.

2.  **Database Schema for Subscriptions**

    We're tracking subscriptions in our Postgres database to avoid depending entirely on external systems:

    ```python
    class Subscription(Base):
        __tablename__ = "subscriptions"

        id: Mapped[uuid.UUID] = mapped_column(primary_key=True)
        user_id: Mapped[uuid.UUID] = mapped_column(ForeignKey("users.id"))
        association_id: Mapped[uuid.UUID] = mapped_column(ForeignKey("associations.id"))

        # Provider-agnostic fields
        status: Mapped[str]  # active, past_due, canceled, incomplete
        plan_name: Mapped[str]  # "monthly", "yearly"
        amount_cents: Mapped[int]
        currency: Mapped[str] = mapped_column(default="EUR")
        interval: Mapped[str]  # month, year

        # Provider-specific fields
        provider: Mapped[str]  # "stripe" or other future providers
        provider_subscription_id: Mapped[str] = mapped_column(unique=True)
        provider_customer_id: Mapped[str]

        # Dates
        current_period_start: Mapped[datetime]
        current_period_end: Mapped[datetime]
        canceled_at: Mapped[datetime | None]

        # Metadata
        created_at: Mapped[datetime] = mapped_column(default=datetime.utcnow)
        updated_at: Mapped[datetime] = mapped_column(default=datetime.utcnow, onupdate=datetime.utcnow)
    ```

    This schema is provider-agnostic, making it easy to swap providers later.

3.  **Webhook Handler Architecture**

    We're using FastAPI's background tasks to process webhooks asynchronously:

    ```python
    @router.post("/webhooks/stripe")
    async def stripe_webhook(
        request: Request,
        background_tasks: BackgroundTasks,
        db: AsyncSession = Depends(get_db)
    ):
        payload = await request.body()
        sig_header = request.headers.get("stripe-signature")

        try:
            event = stripe.Webhook.construct_event(
                payload, sig_header, settings.STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET
            )
        except ValueError:
            raise HTTPException(status_code=400)
        except stripe.error.SignatureVerificationError:
            raise HTTPException(status_code=400)

        # Process event asynchronously
        background_tasks.add_task(process_stripe_event, event, db)

        return {"status": "success"}

    async def process_stripe_event(event: dict, db: AsyncSession):
        event_type = event["type"]

        handlers = {
            "customer.subscription.created": handle_subscription_created,
            "customer.subscription.updated": handle_subscription_updated,
            "customer.subscription.deleted": handle_subscription_deleted,
            "invoice.payment_failed": handle_payment_failed,
            "invoice.paid": handle_invoice_paid,
        }

        handler = handlers.get(event_type)
        if handler:
            await handler(event["data"]["object"], db)
    ```

    This pattern makes it easy to add new event types and providers later.

4.  **Automated Tax Receipt Generation**

    For French associations using Stripe (which doesn't generate Cerfa forms), we're building an automated PDF generator:

    ```python
    from reportlab.lib.pagesizes import A4
    from reportlab.pdfgen import canvas

    async def generate_cerfa_receipt(subscription: Subscription, invoice_data: dict):
        # Create PDF
        pdf_path = f"/tmp/cerfa_{subscription.id}.pdf"
        c = canvas.Canvas(pdf_path, pagesize=A4)

        # Association details (from database)
        association = await get_association(subscription.association_id)
        c.drawString(100, 750, f"Association: {association.name}")
        c.drawString(100, 730, f"SIRET: {association.siret}")

        # Member details
        user = await get_user(subscription.user_id)
        c.drawString(100, 700, f"Bénéficiaire: {user.full_name}")

        # Payment details
        c.drawString(100, 670, f"Montant: {subscription.amount_cents / 100}€")
        c.drawString(100, 650, f"Date: {invoice_data['paid_at']}")

        # Legal text for tax deduction
        c.drawString(100, 600, "Cette attestation vous permet de bénéficier d'une")
        c.drawString(100, 580, "réduction d'impôt égale à 66% du montant du don")

        c.save()

        # Upload to S3/storage
        await upload_receipt(pdf_path, subscription.id)

        # Email to user
        await send_receipt_email(user.email, pdf_path)
    ```

    This triggers automatically when Stripe sends the `invoice.paid` webhook.

5.  **The Dunning Process (Failed Payment Recovery)**

    Smart handling of failed payments is critical. Here's our dunning sequence:

    **Day 0:** Payment fails
    - Stripe automatically retries using Smart Retries
    - Subscription status → `past_due`
    - Send email: "Your payment failed, but don't worry - we'll retry"

    **Day 3:** Still failing
    - Send email: "Please update your card" (link to Customer Portal)
    - Keep access active (grace period)

    **Day 7:** Still failing
    - Send urgent email: "Update card or lose access"
    - Restrict some features (can view but not edit)

    **Day 14:** Still failing
    - Stripe cancels the subscription
    - Revoke all access
    - Send final email: "Your membership has been canceled"

    ```python
    async def handle_payment_failed(invoice: dict, db: AsyncSession):
        subscription_id = invoice["subscription"]
        attempt_count = invoice["attempt_count"]

        subscription = await db.get(Subscription, subscription_id)
        subscription.status = "past_due"

        if attempt_count == 1:
            await send_email(
                to=subscription.user.email,
                template="payment_failed_initial",
                data={"portal_url": create_portal_session(subscription)}
            )
        elif attempt_count == 3:
            await send_email(
                to=subscription.user.email,
                template="payment_failed_urgent",
                data={"portal_url": create_portal_session(subscription)}
            )
            # Restrict features
            await restrict_user_access(subscription.user_id, level="read_only")

        await db.commit()
    ```

6.  **Customer Portal Integration**

    For Stripe users, we're creating portal sessions on-demand:

    ```python
    @router.post("/account/billing-portal")
    async def create_portal_session(
        current_user: User = Depends(get_current_user)
    ):
        subscription = await get_active_subscription(current_user.id)

        if subscription.provider != "stripe":
            raise HTTPException(400, "Portal only available for Stripe")

        session = stripe.billing_portal.Session.create(
            customer=subscription.provider_customer_id,
            return_url=f"{settings.FRONTEND_URL}/settings/billing",
        )

        return {"url": session.url}
    ```

    On the frontend, clicking "Manage Billing" redirects to this URL.

7.  **Multi-Association Platform Fees (Future)**

    When we scale AssoCORE to serve hundreds of associations, we'll implement **Stripe Connect**:

    - Each association connects their own Stripe account
    - AssoCORE takes a small platform fee (e.g., €0.50 + 0.5% per transaction)
    - Money goes directly to the association, minus our fee
    - They manage their own tax liability

    ```python
    # Create platform charge with application fee
    payment_intent = stripe.PaymentIntent.create(
        amount=3000,  # €30
        currency="eur",
        application_fee_amount=80,  # €0.80 (0.5% + €0.50)
        transfer_data={
            "destination": association_stripe_account_id,
        },
    )
    ```

    This makes AssoCORE financially sustainable while keeping costs low for associations.

</Steps>

## Final Recommendation

**For AssoCORE's MVP:** We're implementing **Stripe** as the sole payment provider.

### Why Stripe is the Right Choice

1. **Developer Experience:** The API is exceptional, saving weeks of development time
2. **Customer Portal:** Saves months of building subscription management UI
3. **Non-Profit Pricing:** At 2.2% + €0.25, it's extremely competitive for registered associations
4. **Scalability:** Can grow from 10 to 10,000 associations without changing architecture
5. **International Ready:** When we expand beyond France, it already supports 135+ currencies
6. **Stripe Connect:** Future-proofs us for multi-tenant platform fees
7. **Reliability:** Enterprise-grade webhooks and Smart Retries minimize failed payments

### What About Lemon Squeezy?

We're not implementing Lemon Squeezy for the MVP. The 2x higher fees don't provide enough value for our primarily-French target market. If we expand aggressively into 20+ countries, we'll reconsider, but for now it's over-engineering.

## Conclusion

After weeks of testing and implementation, here are the key takeaways:

1. **Don't build your own billing system.** Even simple subscription logic has edge cases that will eat months of dev time.

2. **Webhooks are harder than they look.** They can arrive out of order, duplicate, or fail silently. Always make handlers idempotent.

3. **Failed payments are inevitable.** Budget at least 10-15% of subscriptions failing each month due to expired cards, insufficient funds, or fraud blocks.

4. **Tax compliance is a nightmare.** Unless you have a legal team, use Stripe for EU/FR or Lemon Squeezy for global.

5. **The Customer Portal is worth its weight in gold.** Saving support tickets alone justifies using Stripe.

6. **Test in Production-Mode first.** Stripe's test mode behaves slightly differently than live mode. We caught three bugs by testing with real cards (immediately refunded) before launch.

<Aside type="caution" title="Security Warning">
**Never log full credit card numbers, CVV codes, or raw card data.** This violates PCI-DSS compliance and can result in massive fines.

Always use Stripe's tokenization system and only store the `customer_id` and `payment_method_id` tokens in your database.
</Aside>
